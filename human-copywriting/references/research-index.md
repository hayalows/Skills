# Research index and evidence weighting

## Scope and method

Research was conducted on 2026-09-06 for the rolling six-month window beginning 2026-03-06. The requested phrase "top 20" does not map to one public YouTube ranking for this topic, so the working sample used recent, search-visible videos selected for relevance first, then visible reach, transcript or chapter usefulness, and creator relevance. This is a defensible research sample, not a claim of an official universal ranking.

Public YouTube search was used before browser research. Where available, visible transcript text was reviewed through YouTube or Tactiq. Several videos did not expose captions or a downloadable transcript in the browser, so the source table records coverage rather than pretending all twenty had complete transcripts. Full verbatim transcript redistribution is intentionally not included. The skill uses paraphrased learnings and source links instead.

The private X portion was performed in the user's already signed-in account, including bookmark search. No password, OTP, or private credential was requested or stored. X results were treated as creator-specific evidence and weighted below patterns that recurred independently across many videos.

## Signed-in X and bookmark research

The signed-in bookmark search covered writing, copywriting, human writing, storytelling, hooks, personal brand, content, AI writing, voice, and selected creators including Dan Koe, Dickie Bush, Justin Welsh, Sahil Bloom, David Perell, Alex Hormozi, and Matt Gray. The most useful public source pages from that private search were:

- [Dan Koe, How to articulate yourself intelligently](https://x.com/thedankoe/status/2011827303962329458): build a small body of core ideas, write to think, use micro-stories, and develop ideas in your own words.
- [Dan Koe, writing building blocks](https://x.com/thedankoe/status/1885331576156020748): pain point, example, personal story, anecdote, statistic, metaphor, quote, advice, and what/how/why as modular material.
- [Dickie Bush, six-question writing warmup](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1785686699864990059): promise, problem, reader, benefit, emotion, and next action.
- [Dickie Bush, 3x3 explanation template](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1767530263456723003): a compact structure for teaching a concept.
- [Sahil Bloom, concise email guidance](https://x.com/SahilBloom/status/2034989524116291987): short, direct, spaced writing is more likely to be read.
- [David Perell, lessons on becoming a better writer](https://x.com/david_perell/status/1773344656606023843): frictionless practice, personality, and a voice that is not interchangeable with generic AI output.
- [Matt Gray, storytelling framework search result](https://x.com/matt_gray_/status/2030085934427828441): story as an attention and meaning structure, not just decoration.

These are not treated as unquestionable rules. A creator's distinctive worldview is evidence of one successful approach; recurrence across independent sources is stronger evidence of a general principle.

## Recurrence weighting

Counts are directional synthesis bands from the sample, not a false-precision statistical estimate. "Very high" means the idea appeared across the major writing, copy, story, and AI-writing lanes. "High" means it appeared across several independent lanes. Tool-specific advice was deliberately discounted.

| Principle | Recurrence | Skill weight | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Write for one reader in one situation | Very high | 5/5 | Relevance beats general cleverness. |
| Use concrete examples, lived detail, and observable consequences | Very high | 5/5 | Specificity is the strongest antidote to generic or synthetic writing. |
| Make the writer's perspective visible | Very high | 5/5 | Experience, beliefs, decisions, and limits cannot be outsourced to a generic model. |
| Clarify the thought and choose a structure before polishing sentences | High | 5/5 | Writing is a way to order thinking. |
| Lead with a real hook, problem, tension, contrast, or promise | High | 4/5 | Earn attention without clickbait. |
| Use simple conversational language and varied rhythm | High | 4/5 | Read aloud and cut inflated phrasing. |
| Turn claims into reader movement: value, proof, objections, action | High in copy lanes | 4/5 | Persuasion is a sequence, not a pile of features. |
| Use story as change: situation, friction, decision, consequence, lesson | High | 4/5 | Transformation gives information emotional shape. |
| Practice, repetition, and feedback compound | Medium-high | 4/5 | A body of work is more useful than chasing novelty. |
| Use AI as editor, critic, or structure partner | Medium-high in AI lanes | 4/5 | Give AI real source material and keep final judgment human. |
| Ban certain words, punctuation, or sentence shapes | Repeated but weak | 1/5 | Useful only as a symptom scan, never as the definition of human writing. |
| Detector-bypass tricks, fake typos, or manufactured randomness | Repeated but weak | 0/5 | Exclude from the skill. They do not create credibility or insight. |
| Income and career promises | Mixed and low-confidence | 0/5 | Exclude from writing rules unless independently verified for a separate task. |

## Resulting operating doctrine

The skill gives the most weight to reader fit, specific detail, visible perspective, clear structure, simple language, and ethical reader movement. It gives less weight to formula worship and almost no weight to detector games. The practical unit of “human-sounding” writing is not a typo or a banned word. It is a clear thought expressed by a particular person for a particular reader, with enough detail to be believable and enough structure to be useful.
