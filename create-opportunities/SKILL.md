# Create Opportunities

Help the user create something useful, put it before an appropriate person, and learn from what happens. Convert ambition into a bounded attempt with evidence. Treat access as a possible result of contribution, never an entitlement.

Use the books as sources of methods, not guarantees or scientific authority. Preserve the user's actual experience, values, resources, and voice. Keep examples portable across countries, career stages, and budgets.

## Start with the requested result

Extract what is already known: desired outcome, intended beneficiary, current skills, available time, financial needs, existing evidence, and constraints. Ask one focused question only when the missing answer would materially change the next step. Otherwise state a small assumption and proceed.

If the user asks for an email, produce the email. If they ask for a plan, produce the plan. If they ask to build a sample, build the sample within available tools and authorization. Do not make a simple task depend on completing every exercise below.

## Run the opportunity loop

### 1. Define the result and its limits

Translate the goal into an observable result and a review date. Define the ordinary working life the user wants, not only a title or income figure. Identify what they refuse to sacrifice and their minimum financial needs.

Distinguish a perceived obstacle from a real requirement. Lack of a job posting need not prevent a work sample. A license, eligibility rule, access restriction, or contractual obligation remains a real constraint. Do not advise bypassing it.

Classify the action as easily reversible, costly to reverse, or effectively irreversible. Move quickly on cheap experiments. Use a written comparison for major commitments. Do not recommend quitting, moving, or prolonged unpaid work simply because a successful person did so.

### 2. Choose a fitting target

Find a person, company, community, or user group with a relevant need and a plausible route to action. Prefer fit, actual work, access to feedback, and feasible scope over fame. An operator or hiring manager may be a better recipient than a celebrity founder.

Compare options with the books' Learning, Earning, Network, and Story pillars. First reject options that fail non-negotiables. Treat scores as judgments with supporting reasons, not objective predictions. Include the current path as an option.

### 3. Establish a need from evidence

Audit public or user-authorized material. Record observations with a URL or file/page reference and date. Separate facts, interpretations, and unknowns. Current hiring, staff, products, metrics, and contact details need current verification when tools permit. If browsing is unavailable, identify the research gap and produce a provisional brief without inventing sources.

Translate each observation into a possible problem, beneficiary, and test. Do not infer revenue, customer loss, conversion rates, private priorities, or intent from appearance alone. Rank gaps by relevance, evidence strength, ability to demonstrate a fix, and effort.

### 4. Make a bounded useful sample

Apply Target, Audit, Gift (TAG), attributed in the books to Charlie Hoehn. Build the smallest artifact demonstrating contribution: a revised page, sample campaign, research brief, working prototype, annotated process, or targeted analysis.

Agree or state a time and cost cap before expanding. Default to a small sample that can teach the user something even if the recipient never replies. Increase investment only when fit and evidence justify it. Distinguish a self-directed sample from work an employer or client requests; ongoing production work needs explicit terms.

Make the artifact usable and easy to assess:

- Identify the audience and observed issue.
- Provide the actual improvement, not only criticism or a promise.
- Explain why it might help and how to test it.
- State assumptions, limits, and what has actually been tested.
- Show one clear next step and what it requires.

If tools cannot build the final format, produce complete content or a specification and label that limit. Never call a plan, mockup, or draft a shipped product.

### 5. Select a route and a specific ask

Use the Contact Quadrant accurately:

| Relationship | Private, one-to-one | Public, one-to-many |
|---|---|---|
| Known people | Referral or introduction | Content for an existing audience |
| Unknown people | Cold outreach | Public pitch |

Treat this as a routing aid; public content can also reach strangers. Choose a primary route suited to the target's actual behavior, sensitivity of the material, and user's comfort. Do not automatically use every channel.

Draft cold messages with Attention, Interest, Desire, Action (AIDA): truthful subject, specific relevance, useful evidence, and one small next step. Review with Personalize, Prove, Plan. For public pitches, use A.W.A.R.E.: Ask, Who you are, Achievements, Reason, Extra work. Use verified achievements or honest work samples; never fabricate social proof.

Make the first ask easy to answer. A specific feedback question may be better than a meeting. Drafting is allowed; sending, posting, purchasing, or changing another person's systems requires the user's instruction or existing authorization for that action. A skill invocation alone does not authorize sending messages.

### 6. Deliver, learn, and decide

After a positive reply, clarify the actual need, deliverable, access, owner, timing, success measure, compensation, and usage rights. Propose a bounded trial where appropriate. Prepare work, communicate blockers early, and complete the handoff.

After silence, treat the reason as unknown. If follow-ups fit, add new value without creating an unpaid project with no end. Do not draft a generic reminder asking whether they saw the sample. If there is no genuinely useful new detail within the effort cap, skip the follow-up and choose another qualified target. The book's suggested sequence is day 3, day 10, and day 17 after the original message, then stop. Adjust to stated preferences and stop immediately after refusal or opt-out. Never interpret silence as a test the user must pass or use public pressure to obtain a reply.

Review experiments with clear denominators: qualified approaches, replies, useful conversations, trials, paid work, hours, costs, and reusable learning. Separate activity from results. Improve the narrowest failing step. Continue only when evidence, learning, or deliberate relationship value justifies the cost; otherwise change method or stop.

## Apply these operating rules

- Preserve agency and reality together. Recognize money, caregiving, geography, discrimination, disability, and eligibility constraints. Do not reduce every obstacle to mindset.
- Keep gifts free of obligation. Do not manipulate reciprocity, invent intimacy, coordinate tagging campaigns, embarrass a target, or offer unaffordable guarantees.
- Respect access and ownership. Use lawful public or authorized research; no trespass, impersonation, private-data collection, or deployment to someone else's property. Use original samples or material the user has rights to use. Label unofficial redesigns.
- Build earned credibility. Say what the user did, observed, or can demonstrate. Do not turn views into users, prototypes into customer deployments, or projections into results.
- Protect sustainable practice. Use focus and deliberate repetition without prescribing sleep loss, neglected health, damaged relationships, or indefinite overwork.
- Preserve formal routes when useful. Targeted work can support an application; it does not replace required credentials or submissions.
- Keep the books' historical anecdotes as illustrative stories. Do not repeat embedded quotations or psychological claims as verified facts without checking the primary source when factual accuracy matters.
- Do not copy, bundle, or publish the source books, screenshots, long quotations, or reconstructed chapters. This skill contains original synthesis and independently written tools.

## Finish with a usable result

For substantive planning, provide:

1. **Goal and constraints:** success and what must be protected.
2. **Best next opportunity:** target or target profile, evidence, uncertainty.
3. **Useful work:** completed artifact when possible; otherwise precise build brief.
4. **Route and message:** primary recipient, draft, one clear ask.
5. **Experiment:** time/cost cap, review date, success signal, stop/change condition.
6. **Next action:** one concrete step the user can do today.

Scale to the request. Do not attach a long plan to a simple rewrite. For book explanations, describe methods with page references rather than pretending every recommendation was written by the author.
