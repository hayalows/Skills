# Research and proof

Use TAG to turn target knowledge into an artifact demonstrating contribution. Keep the recipient's current need central.

## Target

Start with roughly three fitting targets, or one already chosen. Record why each fits, the contribution possible, who owns the issue, the route to them, and unknowns. Do not generate a giant prospect list before testing whether the offer is useful.

For apprenticeships, assess how the person works and teaches. Seek relevant practice and feedback, not only association with success. For clients, assess scope, likely budget, payment reliability, and delivery ability. Do not presume these from social visibility.

## Audit

Use lawful public sources and authorized records. Start with current products, relevant job postings, official interviews, published content, and user workflows. Expand into older material only when it explains a current need. Treat complaints and third-party traffic estimates as leads to validate, not internal facts.

Build a ledger:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Observation | What was seen or stated |
| Source and date | URL, timestamp, or file and page |
| Interpretation | Why it might matter |
| Confidence | High, medium, low, with reason |
| Unknown | What cannot be established |
| Implication | Improvement or question worth testing |

For websites, inspect the task, key information, labels, steps, and friction. A missing price or unclear next step is observable. “This loses 30% of sales” is not observable without data.

For marketing, map audience, offer, distribution, landing page, inquiry step, and subsequent experience. Do not sign up, submit forms, or message anyone unless authorized. Use supplied examples when access is unavailable.

For operations, inspect handoffs, records, exceptions, delays, and decision ownership. Keep private client and employee information out of external samples.

For analytics, define question, population, period, sources, denominator, and calculation. Distinguish counts from interpretations and forecasts. External estimates cannot substitute for verified internal performance.

Keep personal details out unless relevant and publicly volunteered for that purpose. “They said onboarding is a priority” can help; a private address or unrelated family fact should not appear in a pitch.

## Choose the gap

Evaluate each candidate:

1. Does it connect to a current objective or repeated user need?
2. Is the observation supported, with manageable uncertainty?
3. Can the user demonstrate improvement with current capabilities?
4. Can it be completed within the cap and reused as learning or evidence?

Prefer one complete improvement over ten superficial criticisms. If the problem is unknown, provide a narrow hypothesis and seek validation. Do not invent a problem to justify outreach.

## Gift

Select the format that reduces review effort:

| Contribution | Useful sample | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Copywriting | Revised headline and first section | Audience fit, reasoning, proposed test |
| Design | One task-focused screen or campaign asset | Hierarchy, accessibility, task explanation |
| Marketing | Small campaign with usable copy and assets | Audience, objective, sequence, measurement |
| Development | Local working prototype or scoped patch | Functional behavior, test evidence, limits |
| Operations | Checklist, handoff template, exception flow | Concrete scenario and responsibilities |
| Analytics | Decision-ready finding with calculation | Source, denominator, caveats, implication |
| Research | Short sourced brief answering a question | Evidence selection, synthesis, uncertainty |

Set a cap suited to the person. An exploratory sample might take 60–120 minutes with free tools; that is a skill design choice, not a book prescription. Go deeper after qualifying the target. Never turn the author's 50-hour pitch into a minimum standard.

Label speculative work and separate original from proposed versions. Use authorized or original assets. Do not distribute licensed photos, private information, paid-resource screenshots, or another creator's substantial content without relevant rights.

## Package the artifact

Use a short document or small deck when structure helps. Do not require 19 slides because the author used that number. Include:

1. Observed opportunity and source.
2. Intended audience and outcome.
3. Actual revised work or demonstration.
4. Reasoning and assumptions.
5. Test plan and success measure.
6. Scoped next step if interested.

Make review possible without unnecessary account creation. Check permissions before presenting a link, while protecting private material. State draft, mockup, or tested-prototype status.

## Review before outreach

Score each dimension 0 for missing, 1 for partial, 2 for strong:

- **Relevance:** specific evidenced issue for this target.
- **Usefulness:** something assessable or usable immediately.
- **Credibility:** accurate claims, sources, rights, test status.
- **Review effort:** quickly understandable to a busy person.
- **Next step:** clear action and realistic terms.

This is an original quality gate. Aim for at least 8/10 with no zero before calling it ready. Fix false claims and access/rights issues regardless of score. If facts are missing, call it provisional rather than forcing a pass.

## Preserve value after rejection

Save methods, original work, and lessons if requested. Generalize the structure and create a distinct sample for the next target. Remove private and target-specific material before reuse. Rejection does not erase learning, but time and opportunity costs are real. Ask what was learned and whether another attempt deserves investment.
