## What the approval means

Papa Kojo prefers the personal warmth, unhurried pace and connected explanations here over the experimental v1.3 samples, which he found too fast and less personal. He wants the natural voice of v1.2 with easier-to-read words. A later correction also notes that "otherwise" can sound too written when a simpler link would do. In the Absa essay he rejected "That's a number I can point to" as AI-like proof-signposting.

This is a calibration example for thoughtful, medium-length advice to **one person**, not a template to replicate word for word in other contexts. Keep the concern for the recipient, a real explanation of why the advice helps, and room for honest uncertainty. Never invent a private encounter, personal emotion, or testimony to imitate the sample.

## Approved example (full text)

I know you've been trying to stop since you spoke about what began at school. And I know there have been times when you went back to it. You've already been open about it once, and I hope you'll keep talking to people who can help. Asking for help again after a relapse may feel hard, but you shouldn't have to deal with it alone.

One thing I'd ask you to do is look at what tends to happen before you go back. Is it after a long day, when you're alone, or when you're around certain friends? It may be none of those things. That's why I'd want you to think about your own last few weeks. If you can see when the urge gets strong, you can make a plan for that time.

Say you find that evenings are hard. You could make plans with a friend you trust or ask someone to check on you. If the substance is easy to get, ask for help keeping it out of reach. You may need more help than you first thought, and I think that's worth talking about. The plan has to work on the days when you don't feel strong.

Please keep speaking with your bishop. You can also try the Church's Addiction Recovery Program. There are meetings and a guide you can use with support. I'd also want you to speak with a doctor or trained counsellor about the habit itself. Some kinds of withdrawal can be unsafe, so get medical advice if stopping makes you ill.

And if you go back to it again, please don't hide it for weeks. Talk to someone soon. I don't think one relapse tells us all we need to know about your wish to repent. We can take it seriously while still making room for you to keep trying. Look at what led to it and ask what needs to change in your plan.

Keep praying and turning to Christ as you do this. I'd also like you to have one person you can call when things get hard. You can start with that and work on the rest with help.
