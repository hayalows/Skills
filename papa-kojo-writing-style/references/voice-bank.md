# Voice bank: Papa Kojo's own words

This is the most important file in the skill. Read the parts closest to the job before writing.

Only three kinds of evidence go here: words he wrote himself, drafts he approved as his own, and corrections he made. Each entry says where it came from. This repository is public, so private messages appear only as style lessons, never as copied text.

Research on style imitation keeps finding the same thing: a few real samples teach a model a voice better than a long description of it. When this file grows, the skill gets better. See [Slots to fill](#slots-to-fill).

## 1. Public comments, his exact words (LinkedIn, collected 16 Nov 2026)

> So proud of you Muta. It feels good to see you reap the results of your hardwork and resilience. Interesting times ahead. All the best!

> Great stuff, Derrick. I've been enjoying the quiz so far. Learning a lot.

Other short replies he uses: "Learning a lot." "More wins ahead." "Well deserved." With friends he says "brother", "bro" and "big man", and he uses emoji in comments: 🎉 🔥 🥂 🙏🏾 😅.

**What these teach**

- He names the person first, or very early.
- He notices one real thing about them: their effort, their result, the thing they made.
- He looks forward: "Interesting times ahead." "More wins ahead." "All the best!"
- He uses contractions naturally ("I've") and keeps his own spelling ("hardwork"). Do not copy spelling slips on purpose, but do not polish a comment into a press release either.
- Short fragments are normal for him in comments. One exclamation mark is fine.
- He never over-explains a congratulation.

## 3. How he thinks out loud (style notes from his own messages, November 2026)

These come from how he writes and dictates his requests. They show how his mind moves. Most of it belongs in Chat and in the shape of longer pieces, not word for word in polished writing.

- **He thinks in order.** He sets out steps as "first ... and after that ... then". In long pieces, let ideas arrive in a clear sequence with plain sequence words.
- **He says the goal, then the standard.** He names what he wants and then what good looks like ("very natural, easy to understand, flows well"). In writing: point first, then what makes it true or useful.
- **He gives the reason inside the request.** "...because the sound check needs more time." Reasons ride along with asks; they are not saved for a separate paragraph.
- **He says plainly what he does not like.** No drama, no apology. In writing: a disagreement or a "no" can be calm and direct.
- **He checks the listener is with him.** "You get?", "you know", "right?" One light check-in can fit a casual Chat. Never in posts, emails or formal writing.
- **He leans on "very" and "really" for weight, and "maybe" when he is exploring.** Allow one where he would truly stress something. Do not stack them.
- **Spoken fillers stay spoken.** "And all that", "and stuff like that" and repeated "you know" do not go into written prose.
