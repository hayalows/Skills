# The register dial

Pick one register before writing. It settles the small choices that older versions kept arguing about: contractions, emoji, fragments, length, how to open and how to end. If he asks for something different, his request wins.

## The six registers

| Register | Use it for | Contractions | Emoji | Fragments | Usual length and shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Chat** | WhatsApp and texts to friends and family | Yes | One, if the mood fits | Yes | 1 to 4 short lines |
| **Comment** | Replies on LinkedIn, X, Instagram | Yes | Optional, one | Yes | 1 to 3 lines |
| **Message** | Email or WhatsApp to colleagues, clients, organisers, mentors | Light, where natural | No | No | 2 to 4 short paragraphs |
| **Post** | LinkedIn and X posts, captions, newsletter openings | Light, where natural | Rarely | One, if it earns its place | Short paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences |
| **Long form** | Essays, talks, letters of advice, longer newsletter pieces | Natural in personal pieces | No | Rarely | Connected paragraphs that develop one idea |
| **Formal** | CV lines, applications, official letters, bios in third person | Full forms | No | No | Clear, complete sentences; professional words allowed |

"Light, where natural" means: use a contraction when the full form would sound stiff in his mouth ("I'll send it tomorrow"), and keep full forms when the sentence carries weight ("I am sorry this is late").

## How each register opens and ends

**Chat**
- Opens with the answer or the feeling: "Ah, bro, I wish I could." "Congrats, big man!"
- Ends with something easy to reply to, or a warm sign-off: "Let's talk this weekend." "🙏🏾"
- A "no" comes with the real reason in one line and a door left open.

**Comment**
- Opens with the person's name or a direct reaction: "Congrats, Akosua."
- Notes one real thing from their post.
- Ends forward: "More wins ahead." "All the best!" "Well deserved."

**Message**
- First line says why you are writing.
- Give a short reason with each ask, the way he talks: "Could we start at 2pm? The sound check needs more time, and that will help us plan."
- A fact the brief confirms stays confirmed. If the new date is Thursday, say Thursday plainly; do not soften it with "if Thursday works for you" unless the brief leaves it open.
- More than about 60 words: break into short paragraphs.
- Middle gives only the context the reader needs to say yes or act.
- One clear ask. Ends with "Thank you," or "Thanks," and his name when it is an email: "Papa Kojo" for people he knows, "Papa Kojo Mensah" for first contact.
- An apology names what went wrong once, gives the fix and the new date, and does not grovel.

**Post**
- Opens on something the reader can see or recognise: a situation, a cost, a plain observation from the facts. No "Most people...", no question hooks, no "Here is the thing."
- Each paragraph answers the question the last one raised.
- Ends on the most useful line, a small next step, or an honest limit. No moral to finish. No engagement question unless he really wants answers.

**Long form**
- Opens where the reader already is. A situation, a direct claim, or a fair question the reader has.
- Develops through cause, consequence and one real objection. Gives each point room; do not jump from instruction to instruction.
- May keep gentle uncertainty ("I think", "maybe") where it is true.
- Ends when the question is answered: a considered view, an invitation (talks), or a practical next step. Not a recap.

**Formal**
- Opens with the purpose or the strongest true fact for this reader.
- Ties each fact to what the reader needs. No invented results. No "passionate", "uniquely positioned" or "thrilled" without a real reason.
- Ends with a plain next step or a short, sincere line.

## Form notes

| Form | Register | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WhatsApp to a friend | Chat | Answer first. Warm. Easy to reply to. |
| Congratulations comment | Comment | Name, one real thing, forward wish. |
| Condolence | Chat or Message | Short, direct, kind. Say what you will do if the brief says it ("I'll be there on Saturday"). With a church brother or sister, a plain line of faith is natural ("I'm praying for you and your family"); avoid stock phrases like "thoughts and prayers" and "at this time". A simple offer is fine ("Let me know if you need anything before Saturday"). No lesson. |
| Thank-you note | Message | Name the specific thing they did, then say simply what it meant ("It meant a lot that you kept checking on me"). Gratitude in a thank-you note is never an invented feeling; a big story about it would be. Keep it warm, not formal. |
| Invitation or call to act | Post | Name the thing by its real name (for example "Nkra"), tell the reader how to do it well in plain words ("Write what you would want to hear on a hard day"), give the real reason for any rule (safety, privacy), and end with the action. |
| Work or client email | Message | Purpose, context, ask. Polite and direct. |
| LinkedIn or X post | Post | One point, its reason, one consequence or limit. X posts can be one or two sentences. |
| Clarity Before Growth newsletter | Post or Long form | A situation the reader lives in, the cause, the cost, and what this issue will help them do. |
| Brand or service copy | Post | The reader's real problem, what changes, any real proof, one next step. No pressure, no sales-template questions ("Still doing X on paper?"). |
| Application or cover letter | Formal | Verified facts tied to the role. Show how one thing he did connects to what they need. |
| CV line | Formal | Action, object, result only if given. |
| Church talk | Long form | Speakable sentences, verified scripture only, a kind invitation. No borrowed testimony or invented experience. |
| Essay | Long form | One question, honest reasons on more than one side, a real objection, a clear view. |
| Humanise | Same register as the original's purpose | Keep every fact and claim; change only the language. |
| Rewrite of his own note | Same register as his note's reader | Keep his certainty words ("I think", "maybe", "not sure") exactly. |
