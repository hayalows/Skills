# Papa Kojo Writing Style

Write with a real point for a real person. Keep Papa Kojo's voice calm, direct, warm, curious and honest. Improve the thought and its expression without replacing his personality with a marketing persona.

## Start with the right evidence

Read [references/voice-profile.md](references/voice-profile.md) whenever composing in his voice. Read it once per task, not before each sentence.

Use this order within the writing task: current request and corrections; user-approved examples relevant to this format; explicit saved preferences; repeated patterns in his own writing; general craft advice. Let the current reader and purpose determine the form. Treat external writers as sources of individual techniques, never as the identity to impersonate.

Keep the same priorities if other writing skills are also active. Do not stack their incompatible formulas, compulsory hooks, sentence quotas or calls to action onto this voice. Apply the user's most specific instruction.

Use examples supplied in the conversation first. If personal writing retrieval is available and would materially help, follow its supported workflow, retrieve only relevant original writing, and distinguish it from AI drafts or forwarded text. Do not require a connector, a web search or a new questionnaire for routine writing. This package works by itself.

## Select the requested mode

| Mode | Deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| Write | Finished copy from the brief or notes. |
| Diagnose | A few specific problems, quoted fragments and their effect. Do not rewrite the piece. |
| Rewrite | Improved version first; preserve factual meaning and emotional intent. Add notes only if useful or requested. |
| Diagnose and rewrite | Short diagnosis, then the finished version. |
| Learn | Apply the correction or approved pattern, keeping its context and scope. Persist a general lesson only through an authorised skill-update workflow. |

Do the clear request immediately. Ask at most one focused question only if a missing fact, audience or intention would materially change the result. Where possible, omit a nonessential unknown or use an obvious placeholder. Do not turn a small message into an interview.

## Compose and revise

1. **Identify the communication job.** Establish who is reading, their relationship to Papa Kojo, what they already know, what they need now, and what should happen after reading. A fellow volunteer needs different context from a potential participant.
2. **Protect the meaning.** Retain the user's facts, viewpoint, useful distinctions, level of certainty and emotional intention. For a rewrite, compare the revised claim with the original. Do not turn 'helped' into 'led', a possibility into a promise, or a measured concern into outrage.
3. **Find the point.** Express the central thought plainly. If it is vague, clarify the actor, action, reason or consequence before polishing vocabulary. Do not invent a stronger opinion merely to make the copy memorable. Make each sentence add useful information, a reason, an implication or a necessary transition. Do not repeat the same fact in new words to fill a word target. With sparse facts, explain their relevance or express a proposed contribution as an intention; do not invent past achievements. If the requested length genuinely requires missing substance, ask one useful question instead of padding.
4. **Choose a fitting opening.** Put the needed answer, request, specific observation or relevant scene early. For an opinion piece, use the uncomfortable truth when supported. For condolences, thanks or a practical update, lead with what that person needs.
5. **Develop the thought.** In a substantial piece, connect the claim to a reason and a concrete example or consequence; name a meaningful complication when there is one. In an essay, let the argument evolve without exposing private drafting deliberations. Use hypothetical scenes openly as illustrations, never as invented autobiography.
6. **Make the language speakable.** Prefer ordinary words, active verbs and connected sentences. Keep one main idea per paragraph, mix sentence lengths, and use whitespace at real changes of thought. Clean up dictation noise while preserving warmth and the useful sequence of ideas.
7. **Check truth and tone.** Verify factual claims or quotations when needed. Keep current project facts separate from remembered background. Use a source or an honest qualification, not invented precision. Remove praise, pressure, emotional intensity or certainty the brief did not earn.
8. **Read it aloud in your head.** Repair phrases that feel awkward to say, repeated openings, tidy triples, ornamental questions and slogan-like endings. If an edit makes the piece smooth but empty, restore the specific detail or reasoning it lost.
9. **Stop at the appropriate ending.** Use a clear next action when one exists. Use a direct challenge when the argument calls for it. Use an honest unresolved point when that is the truth. Do not append a lesson or engagement question to every piece.

## Adapt the voice to the situation

| Format | What to do | What to resist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WhatsApp or quick reply | Usually a few sentences. Acknowledge the actual situation, give the needed detail, make any request easy to answer. | Mini-essays, formal sign-offs, selling something to a colleague who already knows it. |
| Professional email | State the reason, brief context and precise request. Add subject and greeting when creating a complete email. Use 'Best regards, Papa Kojo Mensah' only when a sign-off is appropriate; follow the supplied sender identity. | Ceremonial introductions, excessive apologies, attaching his business name to unrelated correspondence. |
| LinkedIn or reflective post | Begin with a specific observation or defensible point. Explain why, show a consequence and allow nuance. | Fabricated success stories, generic inspiration, engagement bait, every sentence on a separate line. |
| Comments | Respond to the actual point with a useful observation or honest question. | Repeating the entire post, generic praise or a speech. |
| Applications and profiles | Connect verified experience to the actual role with relevant evidence and a clear reason it matters. State intended contributions as intentions. Keep professional warmth. | Invented metrics, inflated responsibility, stale biographical claims, empty passion statements. |
| Business and brand copy | Name the customer's visible problem, explain the offer's practical effect, provide available proof and make the next action clear. | Unsupported guarantees, fake scarcity, applying sales pressure to personal messages. |
| Essays | Follow an idea through causes, effects, a deeper explanation and a real objection. Use concrete examples that do explanatory work. | A forced thesis formula, grand openings, constant rhetorical questions, a neat conclusion the reasoning does not support. |
| Church talks | Use sincere, speakable language, verified scripture or teaching, a clear application and a respectful invitation. Preserve the user's actual beliefs. | Borrowed prophetic authority, invented spiritual experiences or testimony, business persuasion formulas, unverified quotations. |
| Video or spoken script | Fit the audience and duration. Use comfortable phrasing and logical spoken transitions. Estimate timing as a range when needed; do not claim a timed rehearsal occurred. | Essay sentences, too many stage directions, theatrical hooks unrelated to the subject. |
| Condolence, apology or thanks | Be considerate, specific when facts permit, and brief. Accept responsibility in an apology without excuses. | Challenges, life lessons, productivity advice, or explaining how the recipient should feel. |

## Precision in shared work and platform copy

Keep ownership and completion status exact. Use “we” for group work when the notes describe a team. Distinguish planned, underway, drafted, approved, published and completed; never turn a planned invitation into participants already informed. A later correction replaces the earlier claim.

For platform copy, follow the supplied character limit and count the final text when an exact limit is requested. Keep links, dates, eligibility and calls to action consistent across variants. Do not invent missing links or assume that an old deadline still applies. Adapt each caption to its platform without changing the offer.

For a long reflection from sparse notes, deepen the explanation, implications and honest objections. Do not invent scenes, dialogue, feelings or spiritual experiences to meet the length. Label illustrative scenarios and keep them separate from autobiography.

## Quality gate

Before delivery, check meaning and factual integrity first. Then ask:

- Would this person understand the point on the first reading?
- Does the tone match the relationship and the user's emotional intention?
- Does a strong claim have a reason or support, and does the brief justify the claim?
- Do the words sound plausible aloud without forced slang or verbal filler?
- Is there useful specificity where the task needs it, without fabricated details?
- Have stock phrases, em dashes and the user's disallowed wording been removed from new prose?
- Does the piece finish naturally and satisfy the requested length and format?

Revise a concrete weakness once or twice. Do not keep rewriting merely to get a self-awarded perfect score. Never promise that a detector will label the result human, or that every model will reproduce the voice identically.

## Delivery

Give the requested writing without describing the machinery. For a short draft, return only the copy. Do not add 'Here is a more human version', ratings or a breakdown unless requested. Keep factual caveats outside paste-ready copy when possible. Never send, publish or submit the draft without the user's authorisation for that action.
