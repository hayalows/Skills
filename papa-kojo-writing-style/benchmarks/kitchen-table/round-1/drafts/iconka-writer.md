## B04
I looked at 150 designer portfolios.

It was for a project I call Portfolio Field Notes. Here is what stood out.

The strongest ones told me the problem first. What was broken? Who had the problem? Only then did they show the visuals. By the time I saw the work, I knew why it mattered.

The weaker ones were galleries. Image after image. No context. I could see the screens, but I could not tell what the designer had solved.

And several made me hunt for contact details. Think about that. Someone likes your work and wants to reach you, and they cannot find your email.

So here is my one takeaway. Before you show a single screen, write two or three lines about the problem. What was wrong, and for whom? Then show the work.

Open your portfolio today and look at it like a stranger would. Can you find the problem fast? Can you find your email?

## B05
Your shop is busy every day. So why is there no cash when the supplier calls?

If you run a shop in Ghana, you may know this feeling. Customers come in all day. The shop looks alive. But at the end of the month, you are still short.

Here is the thing. A busy shop and a full cash box are two different stories. Two things usually sit between them.

The first is stock you bought on credit. The goods are on your shelves, but the supplier still wants to be paid. When that bill comes, the cash leaves, even if half the goods have not sold yet.

The second is customers who pay late. You gave them the goods today. They promise to pay next week. Next week becomes next month. On paper, you made a sale. In your hand, you have nothing.

Put the two together and you get a trap. Money goes out to suppliers fast. Money comes in from customers slowly. Your shop looks healthy, but your cash runs dry.

So this week, I want you to look at two numbers. How much do you owe suppliers right now? How much do customers owe you? Write both down. Your missing cash is hiding between them.

## B06
Brothers and sisters, in Exodus 20:8 the Lord says, "Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy."

Look at the first word. Remember. The Lord knew we would be tempted to forget. He knew our weeks would fill up.

And many of our weeks are full. You may have a shop to open, a client waiting, or a report due on Monday. When work is busy, Sunday can start to feel like one more day to catch up.

Here is the thing. The commandment does not wait for a quiet week. It asks us to remember the Sabbath in the busy weeks too. Maybe most of all in the busy weeks.

So how do we do that? We can plan for Sunday before Sunday comes. We can finish what we can on Saturday. We can set the phone down and leave work messages until Monday. We can come to church and take the sacrament with our minds here, with Him.

None of this makes the work go away. Monday will still come. But when we keep the day holy, we show the Lord that He comes first, even in a busy week.

So I invite you to ask yourself one question this week. What is one thing I can set down on Sunday, so I can remember Him?

## B10
You have a salary. It comes at the end of the month. And some months, it runs out before the month does. So a side business starts to look like a good idea.

Should you start one while you still have a job? My honest view: for many young people in Ghana, yes, it is worth trying. But only in the right way, and only at the right time.

Here is the case for it. Your salary gives you a floor. You can test an idea without betting your rent on it. If the business fails, you still eat. If you quit your job to start a business, every slow week can mean a week with no money for food. With a job, you can learn slowly. You can make small mistakes while they are still cheap.

A side business also teaches you things a job may not. You learn to price. You learn to sell. You learn what it feels like when a customer pays late. Those lessons stay with you.

Now the case against. Time is the big one. A side business eats your evenings and weekends. Rest gets thin. Family time gets thin. And if you come to work tired, people will notice.

Here is the objection I take most seriously. Some people say a side business makes you worse at your main job. They have a point. If your mind is on your orders while you sit at your desk, your work suffers. And if your contract says you cannot run another business, you could lose the job that pays your bills. That risk is real. Read your contract before you start.

So when is it not wise? If you have no savings at all, wait and build a small cushion first. If the business competes with your employer, do not do it. If you are already so stretched that you cannot sleep well, adding more will break something. And if you cannot keep the business money apart from your salary, you will never know if it is working.

My advice is simple. Start small. Keep your job steady. Track every cedi that comes in and every cedi that goes out. Give it six months, then look at the numbers with honest eyes.

If the numbers say grow, grow. If they say stop, you have lost little and learned a lot.

So look at your own month. Where would the hours come from, and what would you test first?
