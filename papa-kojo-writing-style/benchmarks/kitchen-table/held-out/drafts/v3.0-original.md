BYU-Pathway students book English Chat times with volunteers, and each volunteer has their own booking page. So to find an open time, students had to open one booking page after another until something fit.

I built a small tool to do that checking for them. It's called English Chat Finder. It looks through many volunteers' booking pages and shows the open English Chat times in one place, with the soonest times first. A student who wants a chat soon can see what is open without clicking through page after page.

I built it because finding a time was taking effort that could go into the chat itself. If a tool can do the clicking, students can spend that time talking.

## H05
Rent is due, so you take some cash from the till. School fees come up, so you take a bit more. A relative calls for help, and the till pays again. Each time it feels fair, because it is your business and it is your money.

Then the month ends, and you cannot say how much the business made. The cash for the house and the cash for stock came out of the same drawer, and nobody wrote down which was which. So when stock runs low and there is no money to restock, it is hard to tell if the business is weak or if it simply paid for too much at home.

Paying yourself a fixed amount helps with this. You choose one amount, you take it on the same day each week or month, and you leave the rest in the business. Your home gets a steady sum it can plan around, and the business keeps cash it can count on. Over time, you can also see what the business really earns.

In this issue, I'll show you how to choose that amount, even when sales go up and down.

## H06
Brothers and sisters, many of us are trying to change something in our lives. Maybe it is a habit we want to stop, or a good one we want to start. And sometimes, when we slip, we are harder on ourselves than we would be on a friend.

King Benjamin gave counsel that I think helps here. In Mosiah 4:27 he said, "And see that all these things are done in wisdom and order; for it is not requisite that a man should run faster than he has strength."

"Requisite" means required. So the verse asks us to work in wisdom and order, at a pace our strength allows. To me, that means steady steps, one after another.

This matters because change takes time. If we expect to be finished in a week, every slip feels like failure, and we may stop trying. But if we remember to move at a pace our strength allows, a slip becomes a reason to rest, learn and start again.

So this week, I invite you to be patient with yourself. Choose one small change, work on it in order, and give yourself the same kindness you would give a friend who is trying.
