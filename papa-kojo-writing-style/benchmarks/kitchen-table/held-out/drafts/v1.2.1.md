## H05
When your business and your own pocket share one till, it gets hard to tell how either of them is doing.

Say you need money for school fees, so you take it from the day's sales. Next week it's transport. Then it's credit for your phone. Each time, the amount feels small. But at the end of the month, you can't say how much the business made, and you can't say how much you took. The two numbers have mixed together.

Paying yourself a fixed amount changes that. You choose a sum, weekly or monthly, and you take that amount and no more. The rest stays with the business.

This helps in two ways. You can see what the business really earns, because your personal spending is no longer hiding inside the sales. And you get a steady figure to plan your home life around, so you're not reaching into the till each time something comes up.

It isn't always easy to start. Sales can go up and down, and some months the business may not support the amount you want. So in this issue, I'll look at how to pick a figure your business can carry, and what to do in the months when it can't.

## H06
In Mosiah 4:27, King Benjamin says, "And see that all these things are done in wisdom and order; for it is not requisite that a man should run faster than he has strength."

He said this just after asking his people to care for the poor, to feed the hungry and to visit the sick. That's a lot to carry. It seems he knew some of them might try to do it all at once and wear themselves out.

I think the same counsel helps when we're trying to change. When we see something in ourselves we want to fix, we often want it fixed by next week. Then we slip, and it can feel like we've failed. Some of us want to give up right there.

King Benjamin doesn't tell us to slow down and stop. In the same verse, he says we should be diligent. So we keep working, at a pace we can keep. That might mean working on one habit at a time instead of five. It might mean asking for help, or resting, or starting again the next morning without listing all the reasons we're not good enough.

Being patient with ourselves is part of doing things in wisdom and order. We can keep trying, and we can be kind to ourselves while we do.
