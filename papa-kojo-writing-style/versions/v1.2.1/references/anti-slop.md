# Human-sounding writing: editing signals, not a detector

Updated for v1.2, 16 Nov 2026. The v1.1 research includes a public catalogue of AI-writing patterns, copywriting advice and Papa Kojo's direct criticism of the sentence: "Most people don't run out of money on a big day. They run out on the small ones." That **particular reversal** felt machine-written to him. Do not infer that every negative sentence is bad or that a scanner knows who wrote a sentence.

## Look for reflexes, not forbidden grammar

| Signal | Why it can feel false | Better editorial question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated "not X, but Y" turns | A dramatic turn can replace an explanation. | Can the true point be stated and explained directly? |
| "Most people...", "we all..." | Claims to know everyone without evidence. | Which real person, event or supported fact is this about? |
| "The secret is...", "Here's the thing..." | Announces insight before providing it. | What changes if the introduction is removed? |
| Tidy threes and stacked fragments | The rhythm feels arranged for effect. | Would the speaker naturally list these things this way? |
| Every paragraph ends with a moral | The conclusion arrives before the idea has had room. | Can the paragraph simply finish? |
| Repeated abstract praise | An adjective stands in for proof. | What can a reader see or check? |
| Made-up details | A fake anecdote may sound vivid but misleads. | Did the user supply this event, number or emotion? |
| Equal-length sentences | The voice can become too even. | Does it sound comfortable when read aloud? |

These are prompts to review. A genuine technical explanation can need a negative distinction. A friendly comment can use a fragment, emoji or exclamation mark. A verified quotation should remain exactly as supplied. Honor the user's explicit avoid-word list separately from generic AI-word suggestions.

## What to do instead

Follow the thought. Show its cause and visible consequence. Prefer plain verbs, accurate dates and genuine context. A concrete example helps only when it is relevant and sourced or clearly hypothetical. Repeat a word if it is the clearest word. Let paragraphs vary only because the ideas vary. A reader may need complexity, disagreement or uncertainty; simplicity must not hide it.

Ask whether the language suits **this** audience and purpose. A CV should sound credible to a recruiter. A church talk can be gentle and reflective. A private reply may be one line. No single voice formula should control all three.

## The checker and its limits

`scripts/voice_check.py` scans a small, explicit set of patterns. It is not a classifier of authorship. It cannot judge sarcasm, context, personal taste, truth, implied emotion or whether a phrase is inside a quotation. Its readability formula uses approximate syllables and can misread names, decimals and specialist terms. Don't change a true claim to make the score lower. A zero-flag result is not a guarantee of naturalness, and a flag is not proof of AI writing.

The dice is also optional. Its role is to help escape a repeated opening, not to make every piece use a different analogy. A grounded reflection with no metaphor may be the best one.
