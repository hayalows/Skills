# Experimental v1.2.1 long-form method (not yet adopted)

**Status:** hypothesis for evaluation, not a promoted voice rule. Keep the approved 361-word one-to-one Word of Wisdom calibration intact. Do **not** treat the 3,052-word Divine Design draft as approved personal voice or a verified testimony. The owner measured that draft at 66.3 FRE, Grade 7.3; the earlier 93.0 sample covered a different topic and length.

## What may be going wrong

Average sentence length was about 13.4 words in both samples, yet reading ease differed sharply. The higher syllable burden of abstract and religious vocabulary likely contributes, but differences of topic and length prevent proving a cause. A stronger evaluation must also examine repeated premises, sections that restart the essay, vague emotion, and sentences that feel written rather than spoken.

**Do not fix this by chopping every sentence into six words.** The user has rejected over-fast, emotionally flat v1.3 passages even when their readability numbers were high.

## Drafting, in stages

1. **Set the honest claim.** Write privately in one or two sentences what the essay can say from the supplied facts and what it cannot know. For Church topics distinguish verified doctrine, personal opinion and unverified interpretation. Never invent a private prompting, testimony or a conversation.
2. **Make a thought map, not a template.** List the actual questions a reader would have, order the answers so each depends on the last, and reserve space for a meaningful objection. A new section should add understanding, not introduce the entire thesis again.
3. **Write with the reader in view.** Use grounded examples only when supplied or clearly hypothetical. Give each point a reason, effect or useful limit. Join sentences based on the real relationship between them.
4. **Check the whole essay before editing individual lines.** Read its first and last sentence in every section. Remove repeated claims; repair sudden shifts; check that the closing answers the question developed rather than declaring a moral.
5. **Edit vocabulary with care.** Prefer everyday verbs and familiar nouns where no precision is lost. Keep accurate names and LDS terms such as *agency*, *repentance* and *eternal life* when they are central, and explain each in context if the reader needs it. Watch for long strings of abstract nouns, even when the sentence itself is short.
6. **Perform the spoken-line check.** Read selected passages from the opening, middle and end aloud. Mark exact phrases that feel like a prepared speech, unsupported emotion or a sentence written to sound wise. Do not flatten every sentence into the same rhythm.
7. **Inspect 400-word windows.** Review the easiest and hardest windows, especially later sections, for vocabulary load, repeated paragraphs and loss of reader connection. Flesch is advisory and cannot judge meaning. A score around 85–93 may be welcome when achieved naturally; no automatic rewrite is required when good doctrine or clear reasoning lowers it.
8. **Final integrity pass.** Check dates, role ownership, private experience, quotes and scripture sources before treating the draft as complete. A clear invented personal memory is worse than an awkward sentence.

## Examples of an edit (illustrative, not endorsed author quotations)

**Less easy:** “Divine guidance does not eliminate the responsibility individuals retain to exercise personal agency in circumstances of uncertainty.”

**Simpler without losing the claim:** “God can guide us, but we still have choices to make. Sometimes we have to act before we know how things will turn out.”

**Thin follow-up:** “This shows the importance of responsibility. It is a lesson we should all remember.”

**Developed follow-up:** “A small mistake in a bank record can hold up someone who needs an answer. Checking the record carefully may seem routine to the worker, but it saves the customer another problem.”

The bank example is general and hypothetical. Do not claim that it happened to the writer.

## How to decide whether to promote this guidance

Compare **v1.2, v1.2.1, v1.3 and v1.4** on the eight tasks in `benchmarks/longform/tasks.json`. Keep the model and settings fixed; require three distinct model calls per version/brief. Rate voice, emotional honesty, sentence craft, coherence and source fidelity with exact supporting quotes. Audit all sentences in the two finalist essays for a held-out prompt. Use the owner's blind preference as an explicit voice criterion. If the change only raises FRE while reducing thought, sincerity or factual care, reject it.
