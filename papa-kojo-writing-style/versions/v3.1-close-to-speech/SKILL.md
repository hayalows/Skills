# Close to Speech

Write for the person Papa is speaking to. Keep his point, his level of certainty and the relationship in view. A finished piece should sound like something he could say and stand behind.

Read [his voice bank](../../references/voice-bank.md) for evidence and [his approved essay](../../references/approved-refined-v12.md) for longer pacing. Use the [register guide](../../references/register-dial.md) when the setting affects the choice of words. The current request and corrections come first. Treat dictation as evidence of what he means and what he stresses. Clean up recognition errors and repeated starts. Carry over a familiar phrase only where it belongs. Material from emails or private chats stays private. A sent draft of uncertain authorship can establish context, but cannot establish his voice.

## Choose what the draft needs

- **Light edit.** Find the line he could already send. Keep the words that carry his feeling, meaning or relationship. Fix the point where a reader would stumble. Leave a good sentence alone.
- **Message or email.** Lead with what the reader needs at this point in the exchange. Acknowledge what they already did. Make one clear request when the brief calls for one. A time estimate keeps its uncertainty. A promised time stays a promise. Match the warmth to the person.
- **Personal statement.** Start with a real action or choice, or a plain reason for applying. Let the scene lead to an explanation of what the applicant did, what he can honestly say changed, and how that connects to the programme. A small confirmed action is enough. If an essential event, personal role or outcome is missing and the piece depends on it, ask one precise question. Where no answer is needed, write from the facts and let the limit stand. Never fill a gap with a reconstructed memory or dialogue.
- **Post, talk or essay.** Say the main thought in plain words to one reader before drafting. Follow it through one concrete example, a reason and any limit that matters. If a paragraph feels detached, carry a detail, question or consequence forward from the paragraph above. Let a qualification change the argument where it should. End at the last sentence that adds something.
- **Diagnosis.** Point to the exact passage and what it changes for the reader. Rewrite only when asked.

Before sending, check actor, action, numbers, dates, source, ownership, certainty and required length. Preserve a stated word range. In a study or report, state what was measured and the strongest limit when they change the interpretation. Prefer active verbs and familiar words. Keep a necessary technical term and explain it in plain English once. Use British and Ghanaian English unless the brief says otherwise. Keep names and exact quotes. Follow the avoid list in the [voice profile](../../references/voice-profile.md). Read aloud once to catch stock contrasts, uniform rhythm and any note of praise the facts have not earned.

## Keep longer writing easy to read

Aim near Grade 5. For short messages, check whether the reader can immediately tell who did what, what happens next and what remains uncertain. A two-sentence note can sound exactly right with no numerical grade.

For an essay or statement, draft from a short outline where each section has one job. Read each section for its strongest point and the connection into the next one. Use `python3 scripts/reading_windows.py < draft.txt` from the skill root on the prose. Review difficult windows. Explain the hard term, split a sentence only where the thought naturally turns, and read the section again for flow. Compare the whole estimate and the spread across sections before and after. Preserve quotes, precise terms and real meaning even when they lift the estimate. If a grade is requested, disclose the estimate and its limits. Treat the reader's understanding as the test.

## Learning from corrections

Apply the latest correction to the requested piece. Carry it into other work only at the level Papa approves, such as one recipient, one form or a general preference. An approved AI draft is evidence of a style preference. It is never evidence of a lived experience.
