# Composition and content

## Choose a page structure

| Content need | Useful starting structure | Main risk to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One clear offer with a strong asset | Split hero | Content order on small screens |
| A short editorial thesis | Typographic hero | Readability and a clear next step |
| Related capabilities or projects | Grid with meaningful grouping | Repetitive filler or forced empty cells |
| Specifications and comparisons | Semantic table or grouped list | Horizontal overflow and hidden key details |
| A sequence or process | Ordered steps | Decorative numbering without real order |
| Rich work samples | Gallery with captions | Keyboard access and image cost |
| Pricing or eligibility | Direct comparison and plain requirements | Concealed costs, exclusions or invented urgency |

These are options, not a required page inventory. Preserve approved navigation and search-relevant content during redesigns. If a route or tracking contract must change within the requested scope, include migration and verification.

## Hierarchy and rhythm

Choose a focal point for each section and use alignment, size, spacing and grouping to express importance. Use labels only when they add information. Keep related elements close and allow real changes of subject to create pauses. Avoid decorative metadata that implies unverified dates, locations, product versions or availability.

Prefer a readable natural headline over a rigid two-line target. Account for zoom, long words and different writing systems. Repeated layouts are appropriate for comparable items; avoid adding arbitrary variation that makes comparison harder.

Keep essential price, eligibility, risks and next-step information available before commitment. Progressive disclosure suits secondary detail, not a surprise cost. A carousel should not be the only way to access critical content.

## Truth and voice

Use plain, concrete language. Label controls by their real action and keep success feedback consistent. Explain what failed and how to recover. Do not invent proof or convert intended functionality into a shipped claim.

Preserve exact quotations, names and technical meaning. Shorten a testimonial only through a faithful excerpt; never silently rewrite it inside quotation marks. For Papa Kojo’s authored copy, follow his supplied voice preferences and avoid em dashes in new prose.

Show mock data as mock data to the viewer when it might be mistaken for a real metric; an HTML comment alone is insufficient. Keep decorative illustrations separate from evidence of a real customer, product or outcome.

## Redesign review

Record what works, what fails and why before changing it. Prioritize the problem: a font change cannot fix unclear eligibility, and animation cannot fix a broken primary action. Keep content, URL structure, brand assets and working interactions unless changing them is within scope. Compare before and after using the same task and viewport where practical.
