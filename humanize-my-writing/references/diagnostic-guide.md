# Natural-writing diagnostic guide

Adapted from the principles behind Hardik Pandya's MIT-licensed `stop-slop` project. Use judgment. A phrase is evidence only when it weakens the writing in context.

## Diagnose in this order

### 1. Meaning and specificity

Flag claims that announce value without showing it:

- “The implications are significant.”
- “This is a powerful solution.”
- “The stakes are high.”

Ask what changed, for whom, under what conditions, and why the reader should care.

### 2. Voice and distance

Flag writing that speaks from nowhere, hides the actor, or sounds unlike the user. Replace “businesses face challenges” with the actual person, pressure, or event when known.

### 3. Canned language

Watch for throat-clearing and filler such as:

- “Here’s the thing”
- “It is important to note”
- “In today’s fast-paced environment”
- “At its core”
- “Let that sink in”
- “Moving forward”
- “When it comes to”

Cut the setup and state the useful sentence. Keep a familiar phrase when it sounds natural in the user's context and does real work.

### 4. Formulaic structure

Watch for repeated patterns:

- forced binary contrasts: “It is not X. It is Y.”
- dramatic fragments: “Trust. Growth. Results.”
- automatic groups of three
- rhetorical questions answered immediately
- every paragraph ending with a punch line
- headings that merely repeat the next sentence
- repeated “This is where…” transitions

Change the structure, not only the vocabulary.

### 5. Rhythm

Read aloud. Flag several sentences with the same length or syntax, too many fragments, overlong sentences with stacked clauses, and excessive one-line paragraphs. Vary the pace according to meaning.

### 6. Trust

Remove fake confidence, unnecessary reassurance, inflated praise, and instructions telling the reader how to feel. Support claims or narrow them.

### 7. Density

Cut repetition, meta-commentary, and sentences that only announce what the next section will do. Do not cut examples, context, or emotional detail that helps the reader understand.

## Avoid rigid editing

- Do not ban every adverb. Remove one only when it adds no precision.
- Do not ban passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally backgrounded.
- Do not force every sentence to have a human subject.
- Do not replace one cliché with another.
- Do not make all prose terse. Natural writing can be expansive, hesitant, playful, or formal.
- Do not treat a detector score as proof that text was written by AI.

## Scoring rubric

Score each area from 1 to 10:

| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| Meaning | Does every important line say something clear and supportable? |
| Voice | Does it sound appropriate for this user, audience, and setting? |
| Specificity | Can the reader picture the people, action, or consequence? |
| Rhythm | Does the prose move like speech without becoming messy? |
| Trust | Does it respect the reader without hype or hand-holding? |
| Density | Can anything be cut without losing meaning, tone, or useful context? |

Treat scores as editing prompts, not scientific measurements. Name the two or three biggest problems first.

## Recommended diagnosis format

Start with a plain verdict. Then show the highest-impact evidence:

| Pattern | Evidence | Why it feels artificial | Better direction |
|---|---|---|---|

End with a short priority list. Add a rewritten version only when requested.
