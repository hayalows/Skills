# Reply workflow: continue, do not restart

Use this reference for WhatsApp threads, screenshots, quoted messages and student follow-ups. **REPLY is the default task** when an existing conversation is supplied.

## Read the timeline

Identify:
- Original date, later date and local time for each actual message.
- Which side sent it. A copied passage under the student's name may be a WhatsApp quote of the mentor's earlier text.
- The newest *new* information from the student. Do not mistake the quoted text for a fresh concern.
- What the mentor already asked, answered, promised, offered or followed up.
- Whether the student answered a previous question, accepted a follow-up, declined help or left a specific issue open.
- Whether a deadline or relative date was anchored to an older message. Use exact dates if helpful, especially after several days.
- Whether a late reply or broken promise requires an apology and concrete repair.

If a screenshot is cut off or ownership is unclear, do not invent a missing reply. Ask one brief question only if the distinction materially changes the outcome.

## Intent and reasoned next move

Do not write a response until you can privately complete this sentence: **"The student is trying to ___; the main barrier or question is ___; because the mentor has already ___, the next useful move is ___."** If part is unknown, mark it unknown rather than inventing it.

Read beyond the literal phrase without over-reading emotions. For example, "I need a job" might be about tuition, time, transport, experience or immediate cash; the thread determines which. "I already did that" means the mentor must move past the finished step, not re-explain it. "I withdrew" is a completed decision, so stop offering ways to avoid it and address what comes next. "Your reply came late" signals an unmet relationship commitment; acknowledge and repair it before moving to a new pitch.

Use these lenses, only when grounded:
- **Aim:** What progress or decision matters to the student now?
- **Stage:** What has been tried, applied for, received, declined or completed?
- **Barrier:** What actually stops progress? Distinguish a guess from a fact.
- **Urgency:** Is there a verified deadline, safety risk, locked assignment or consequence of waiting?
- **Authority:** What can the student do, what can the mentor do, and what needs an instructor, support or Finance?
- **Commitment:** Has the mentor promised to check something? Research and give a real update before offering another vague promise.
- **Agency:** What one doable next step gives the student a meaningful choice?

Do quiet, authorised research before asking the student to repeat something you can reliably verify yourself. Ask one focused question only when its answer changes the best next step. Don't make the student answer five eligibility questions in one message.

## Select the response type

| Student's latest meaningful message | Mentor's next move |
| --- | --- |
| Answers a question about what they have done | Build on it. Ask only what changes the next step. |
| Asks a direct question | Answer it first, with verified details. |
| Gives a difficulty | Attend to the specific difficulty, then help identify one feasible action. |
| Shares good news | Respond naturally, without generic congratulatory padding. |
| Accepts an agreed follow-up | Confirm the agreement. Don't ask for agreement again. |
| Says they felt unsupported | Acknowledge the delay or failure once, repair the concrete issue, and avoid defending the mentor. |
| Says "thank you" with nothing outstanding | A brief acknowledgement or **no reply needed yet**, depending on the exchange. |
| Shows an open, older question the mentor did not answer | Answer or repair that omission before starting a new topic. |
| Gives no new message after mentor has replied | **No reply needed yet.** Suggest a relevant future follow-up only if warranted. |

## Message craftsmanship

Start at the exact place the thread stopped. Do **not** begin with the student's name, "Good to hear", "Oh okay", "I see", "I understand", "Thank you for explaining", or any acknowledgement by reflex. Such words are fine only when they are genuinely the most natural next thing to say.

Don't restate a student's words to perform empathy. Acknowledging facts can be useful when the student is sick, bereaved, frightened or disappointed, but keep it grounded. If the next step is simply "Have any employers contacted you yet?", write that question, not two paragraphs of reassurance.

When the student answers an earlier question:
1. Treat the answer as new evidence.
2. Decide if it completes the task, changes the recommendation, or reveals another obstacle.
3. Respond with the next relevant action or question; do not ask the same one in new words.

Prefer one main point in a short message; two short paragraphs when context genuinely helps. **Every outgoing draft must use Papa Kojo Writing Style V3.0 Original**, with the Message register, approved voice evidence and the spoken-aloud test, not just a superficial casual tone. No signature or repeated greeting during the same thread. Use their name sparingly, for a genuine purpose, not every turn.

## REACH in the moment

- **Relate:** The student's goal directs the next question. Don't force a name or rapport statement.
- **Engage:** Care may be shown by taking the concern seriously and doing something useful. Not every reply needs an emotional phrase.
- **Advance:** Offer a small real step, such as reviewing Canvas deadlines, contacting an instructor through Canvas Inbox, checking a portal item, or reviewing an official support route, as permitted.
- **Commit:** When meaningful, propose a concrete check-in date or time window and seek agreement. If they have already agreed, confirm it once and keep it.
- **Honor:** Help students make informed choices without implying that the mentor decides grades, finance awards, device allocation or employer selection.

The framework helps the **whole interaction**. A single line can satisfy the immediate task even if it does not use every REACH element.

## Timing and follow-ups

Don't invite a long conversation late at night if the student can reasonably respond tomorrow. When an urgent deadline is close, say so only after checking the correct official date. Distinguish:
- "Could I check with you tomorrow evening?" = proposed, not booked.
- "Yes, I'll be glad to have you check on me" = agreement received; confirm and follow through.
- "I'll find out and update you" = mentor promise; check sources and send a substantive update, not another promise to check.
- "Please contact your instructor" = student suggestion, not proof it happened.
- "I sent it" = a fact only if the user confirms sending.

Don't automatically offer a 30-minute meeting. A single answer by text can be enough.

## Smart assistance and official evidence

When a useful response needs policy, a step-by-step procedure, a funding route, a deadline, or an institutional claim, use [source-finding.md](source-finding.md) and [policy-checks.md](policy-checks.md). Find and read the best current primary article before composing. If the student asks a yes/no policy question, start the reply with the verified answer, not a question about feelings.

Show the mentor a short source note **after** the WhatsApp copy: article title + KA number + working URL, what that source supports, any block-specific condition still to check, and the next authorised action. Don't paste an entire article into the reply. If no primary source could be verified, say so and avoid claiming certainty.

## Output rules

Default: copy-ready WhatsApp text **first**. If the user asks only for a reply, skip the analysis or keep it to one short relevant note. For an explicit diagnosis, explain the exact issue and effect; do not rewrite unless asked. For a draft revision, preserve facts, responsibility and certainty from the supplied thread.

Never fill missing factual details with memories from an unrelated student or case.
