# Conversation intelligence and intent-state decisions (v1.2)

Use for fictional, permitted de-identified context only. Don't pass real protected full threads or identifiers into unauthorised AI. A thread may contain multiple goals, a reply quote, a period of silence and a mentor promise. **Reason about what changed**, not what the last sentence looks like.

## Build a quiet state ledger

For each meaningful topic privately track: **who said it, date/time, stated goal, completed action, pending action, last reply owner, promised mentor action and deadline, authoritative source needed**. Treat each as Known / Inferred / Unknown. Do not expose this ledger to a student. Preserve differences between student intention, confirmation, and system verification.

Chronology is per message date, not the current day. WhatsApp quoted text is older evidence, not a new independent message; read receipts and emoji are not evidence of completing a task. A screenshot cut off at the bottom cannot prove no later reply.

## Ask seven questions privately

1. **Literal:** What did the student ask or report? Did they answer the mentor's previous question?
2. **Goal:** What outcome do *their words* support, as distinct from an assumption?
3. **Stage:** What is already done, attempted, refused, waiting, or no longer possible?
4. **Main barrier:** Is the issue learning, a technical fault, finances, time, a decision or something urgent? Multiple barriers may coexist.
5. **Authority:** Who can act: student, instructor/grader, Support, adviser, Finance, HSEF/interviewer, mentor or MSA?
6. **Promise:** Did the mentor already offer to check, send, call or follow up? Was it delivered or delayed?
7. **Decision:** What is the smallest useful next action? Would another question change it?

## Route the next action

| State | Next action | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh, unanswered student question | Answer from known/verified source; if impossible ask one essential fact | Generic weekly greeting |
| Student reports completed action | Progress to next stage; check outcome only if relevant | "Have you tried X?" again |
| Student says thanks/👍 with no open item | Small acknowledgement or **no reply needed yet** | New check-in to extend chat |
| Mentor promised update; student says "okay" | No needless reply, **keep pending commitment** | Marking promise complete |
| Mentor was late and student says so | Admit delay once; **do the promised check now**; explain impact and next choice | "I'll check" again without checking |
| Student withdrew / made final decision | Respect completed decision, verify what comes next | Advice aimed at undoing past decision |
| Student reports same failed troubleshooting | Acknowledge the attempted step; move to the *next* verified procedure | Recycling the same login tips |
| Student wants job to pay tuition | Recognise tuition as possible aim; ask only grounded next step | Assuming job-placement authority |
| Strong fear, danger or self-harm cue | Immediately follow approved crisis assessment / urgent escalation | Treating it as ordinary mentoring |
| Student declines contact or help | Respect preference; follow current authorised rules | Re-engagement by pressure |
| Contacted student by email, student replies there | Continue in that channel unless preference changes | Switching to WhatsApp by default |

Two simultaneous questions: answer both if short and verified, otherwise prioritise the most urgent practical issue, acknowledge the other and set an honest follow-up. "Yes, but..." may **update** prior agreement rather than confirm it.

## Question budget and speech

One focused question only when necessary. A direct answer needs zero. Offer one doable next action rather than a list of options. Don't force a positive remark, a name, apology or ending question. A student's real obstacle can justify a simple empathetic sentence; do not invent an emotional backstory.

## Closure with state conservation

**No reply needed yet** does not mean **nothing to do**. Keep an already agreed follow-up, a ticket awaiting verification, or a pending Mentor Portal task in the mentor's authorised workflow. Distinguish student *replied* (including emoji) from *reply significance* (emoji-only, moderate, substantial). A new emoji cannot prove an account is fixed.

## Tiny adversarial examples

- The student quotes "Have you registered?" then writes "I registered yesterday." The new message is the registration **answer**. Move forward.
- Mentor wrote "I'll send the guide Friday"; student replies "Okay". Don't ask for another confirmation, but Friday remains outstanding.
- A student writes "Thank you. Also, my grade is wrong." That is **not closure**. Address the grade query.
- After "I withdrew yesterday", don't send instructions to prevent withdrawal. Verify current post-withdrawal options.
- "I messaged the instructor and no answer" means the contact step is complete. Ask when and check the next official route if timing demands it.

## Audit of an actual draft

Identify the exact sentence in the thread which justifies the reply. If none does, reconsider whether a reply is required. Look for repetition, false claims about completed work, hidden assumption, wrong student programme, unfulfilled promise or change of sender.
