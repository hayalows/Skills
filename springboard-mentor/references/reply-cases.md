# Fictional reply cases and regression checks

All cases below are **invented examples** for testing the skill. They are not real student records, approved messages or institutional policy.

## 1. Student completed a registration

**Mentor already asked:** "Have you completed your job profile, or are you still setting it up?"

**Student:** "I've done that."

**Good continuation:** "Have you heard back from any employers yet, or are you still waiting?"

**Avoid:** "Good to hear, [name]. I understand why you're looking for work ..." This restarts and repeats the topic rather than acting on the answer.

## 2. Student accepts a follow-up

**Mentor:** "Would it be okay if I checked in tomorrow evening?"

**Student:** "Yes, please."

**Good continuation:** "I'll check in tomorrow evening as agreed. Take care tonight."

**Avoid:** Asking "Would tomorrow evening be okay?" again; treating a proposed meeting as already completed.

## 3. Student says the mentor was late

**Student:** "I already made the decision because you replied too late. I didn't feel supported. How does the assistance process work?"

**Good pattern:** Own the late reply in one sincere sentence, answer the direct question using current official guidance, and offer a concrete next step or a specific promised update.

**Avoid:** Defending the delay, repeating advice for a decision already taken, or saying "I'll check" again without doing the check.

## 4. Student expresses anxiety but gives no root cause

**Student:** "I'm trying, but keeping up with assignments is hard and I worry about repeating."

**Good continuation:** One question that helps distinguish which course or main barrier needs attention, or one useful next action if the thread already identifies it.

**Avoid:** A generic motivational paragraph, promises that everything will work out, or a new weekly reminder.

## 5. Quoted message is not the student's new content

**Mentor earlier:** "Could you check your grades?"

**Student replies quoting that text, then adds:** "I checked. One course is at 70%."

**Good continuation:** Address the 70% course and relevant verified options, not the quoted question as though the student just asked it.

## 6. A direct scholarship question

**Student:** "How do I receive the Mentor Bridge discount?"

**Good pattern:** Explain verified referral process and relevant eligibility checks directly; ask only for the most useful missing fact. Do not suggest a direct student application if current official rules require a mentor recommendation.

**Avoid:** "I understand why tuition is difficult for you" as the main answer or a promise of an award.

## 7. No pending reply

**Mentor:** "Thanks. I'll call you on the agreed day."

**Student:** "Okay."

**Possible output:** "No reply needed yet. Follow through on the agreed day."

**Avoid:** Adding a new question or meeting invitation solely to keep the chat active.

## Review rubric

Test the proposed text against five questions:
1. **Timeline:** Does it respond to the newest actual student content?
2. **Non-repetition:** Does it avoid all questions and explanations the mentor already covered?
3. **Natural voice:** Could Papa Kojo say it aloud without a template tone or needless student's name?
4. **Progress and promises:** Is the next step proportionate, and are plans kept separate from actions?
5. **Policy and privacy:** Are consequential facts verified, within role, and handled without protected data?

Any failure merits revision before presenting the draft.
