# Narrative and design reference

## Chapter pattern

Use this sequence for every substantial section when it fits the evidence:

1. **Puzzle:** Open with a tension, surprise, or practical question.
2. **Framework:** Give the reader a useful short way to think about the issue.
3. **Proof:** Use one or two charts, comparisons, or concrete examples.
4. **Grounding:** Add a checkable historical analogy or named real-world situation if it makes the abstract idea easier to picture.
5. **Nuance:** State the exception, dependency, limitation, or disconfirming evidence.
6. **Transition:** Make clear why the next question follows.

## Visual system

### Palette

- Accent: one saturated brand colour for section numerals, the highlighted chart series, stat callouts, thin rules, and numbered badges. Do not use it for body copy.
- Ink: near-black such as `#1A1A1A` for text.
- Support: light grey panels around `#F2F2F2`–`#F5F5F5`, and a mid-grey such as `#6E6E6E` for notes, captions, and comparison data.

Treat whitespace as part of the system. Keep generous margins and do not fill empty areas merely because they are available.

### Typography

Use the approved brand typography. When no brand system exists, this two-family pairing is an optional starting point:

- An editorial serif, such as Georgia, PT Serif, Source Serif, or Charter, for report titles, chapter titles, and pull-stat figures.
- A clean sans-serif, such as Inter, Arial, Helvetica, or Source Sans, for body copy, charts, captions, footnotes, and navigation.

Keep body copy around 10.5–12 pt in reports. Use short paragraphs and enough leading to make detailed reading easy.

### Reusable visual devices

- **Section numerals:** Pair a large accent-coloured 01, 02, 03, and so on with the chapter title.
- **Pull statistics:** Make an important number large, with a short bold line explaining why it matters. Do not bury the headline number in a paragraph.
- **Declarative charts:** Write the conclusion in the chart title. Use the accent colour for the main series and grey for the comparison. Direct-label values where practical.
- **Framework boxes:** For steps, categories, or principles, use light-grey panels with small numbered accent badges, a bold lead phrase, and a plain-language explanation.
- **Header and footer:** In reports, use a small title, page number, thin divider rule, and legible source notes consistently.
- **Photography:** Use one strong, relevant image at a chapter opener or key transition. Avoid random stock-image decoration.

## Recommendation design

Do not end with generic advice. Divide the action page by who must act, such as leadership, operations, investors, or customers. Give each group a short numbered list with a bold lead action and an explanation of why it matters.

## Content checklist

- Does the executive summary express the thesis and all material findings?
- Does every section advance the decision with a useful question, finding or claim?
- Are named frameworks useful, clear, and based on real evidence?
- Does every chart title say what the data means?
- Does every major section acknowledge a limitation or exception?
- Is the evidence traceable to a source or clearly marked as an assumption?
- Does the ending give distinct people specific next actions?
