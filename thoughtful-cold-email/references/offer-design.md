# Offer, response, and economics

## Source and scope
The Hormozi GPT plugin instructions identify $100M Offers (value equation), $100M Leads (outreach and lead magnets), and $100M Money Models (economics and delivery) as its operating frameworks. The following are practical adaptations of those concepts to cold email, not direct quotations or claims of independently verified performance. For one email use a light mental pass; for campaigns use the full plan. Noncommercial work need not imitate a sales funnel.

## Value before copy
1. Recipient and context: who can act, what do they do, and what evidence supports the fit? Distinguish fact, hypothesis, and unknown. A first line is useful only if it leads to the proposal.
2. Desired outcome: state one result the recipient plausibly values in their terms; do not presume the person needs or wants it. Replace generic ‘collaboration’ with a tangible option.
3. Confidence: show a relevant work sample, credible process or verified result; no fabricated clients, statistics, authority or endorsements. An early project can offer a concrete resource or ask for focused feedback; it must not imply proof of outcomes.
4. Recipient cost: clarify what the first step involves, including time and effort where known. Make it easy to evaluate or decline; do not shift substantial unpaid work to them. Do not invent delivery speed, deadlines, scarcity, guarantees or urgency.
5. Offer a real contribution: a relevant resource, specific idea, candidate guest topic, voluntary help, or sample. Make it useful independently and appropriate to context. Do not suggest services outside the sender’s capacity or add unnecessary attachments and links. For feedback, narrow to one question; acknowledge expert work can warrant compensation without promising it.
6. Select a next action: a specific reply, permission to share a useful idea, a focused review question, or a short conversation if genuinely warranted. Only one main ask. Do not use a fake feedback request to conceal a sales pitch. Make value reciprocal for partnerships and ask for no personal or sensitive data.

## Examples: hypothetical, no claimed results
- Weak agency pitch: ‘I do branding. Can we meet?’ Stronger if verified: ‘Your new [product] is sold across [channels]. I can put together a short visual consistency review covering [two specific items]. Would it be useful if I sent it?’ Only offer work the sender is willing and able to do.
- Weak community pitch: ‘Let’s collaborate.’ Stronger: ‘Your [verified programme] supports [audience]. We made a free [relevant guide] for [specific situation]. Could I send it for your team to consider sharing?’ Do not claim their audience needs it or that it has clinical validation.
- Weak request for expertise: ‘Review our website.’ Stronger: ‘Your work on [specific topic] caught my attention. Would you be willing to answer one question: [focused question]?’ Even a small request is an ask, not an implied benefit to the expert.

## Pilot and diagnosis
Record each recipient, source, actual offer version, send and follow-up dates, result (positive, negative, referral, out-of-office, bounce, no reply), next action and useful outcome. Pilot a small relevant set; test one substantive difference where comparable, randomize where feasible, record denominators and allow enough time for replies. A suggested 10–20-person pilot is a learning exercise, not a statistically decisive test or target. Review deliverability, audience fit, offer, trust, message and reply handling in that order when evidence supports it; do not blame copy by default. Define qualified replies and desired outcomes in advance. A low n, missing tracking, confounding, delayed replies and differences between people make results uncertain. Stop on objections, unsubscribes, complaints or provider warnings.

## Commercial unit economics: only when applicable
For a commercial campaign, establish actual or clearly labeled hypothetical GH₵ values before paid acquisition or scale: revenue collected per customer; gross profit = revenue minus direct delivery cost; contribution profit = gross profit minus other variable costs; CAC = acquisition spend divided by new customers (include relevant labour where possible); break-even CAC = expected contribution over the customer relationship before acquisition; LTV:CAC = expected contribution lifetime value divided by CAC; payback = CAC divided by contribution generated per period. State time horizon, retention, refunds, defaults, fixed costs and capacity. Check upfront cash versus costs, owner salary, downside CAC, conversion and delivery costs. State unknown if inputs are missing; do not fabricate a pass/fail. Any 3:1 ratio or 30-day payback is a heuristic from plugin instructions, not universal evidence or required for unpaid relationship-building. For free public-health outreach track staff time, capacity, mission outcomes and privacy, not revenue or upsells. Never turn clinical care into a sales tactic.
