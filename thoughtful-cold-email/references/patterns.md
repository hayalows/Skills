# Purpose-specific patterns

These are reasoning patterns, not copy-paste scripts. All bracketed facts need confirmation. Make the proposed action match the relationship and recipient's workload.

## Client or freelance work
Specific context → plausible opportunity, framed as a hypothesis → specific service and credible evidence → ask permission to share one useful idea or discuss briefly. Example: 'I noticed [verified detail]. I help [audience] with [specific work]; [truthful work sample]. Would it be useful if I shared one idea for [goal]?' Do not describe a design as broken without grounds or promise revenue.

## Partnership or community
Shared purpose → how the proposed collaboration could help both audiences → realistic contribution and constraints → ask whether they are open to a short conversation or specific next step. Do not imply endorsement or affiliation.

## Job or career conversation
Specific role/team and reason for relevance → verifiable experience or work → ask a focused question, portfolio feedback, or a short conversation. Follow the employer's application process and avoid framing a request for a job as an unrelated research interview.

## Editorial, backlink, or podcast
Identify the existing page or episode and why a particular resource or discussion genuinely helps its audience → describe the original useful material → ask the editor or host to consider it. No entitlement to a link or fabricated listenership.

## Research and expert help
State the subject, why this person's published expertise is relevant, what information is requested, how it will be used, and a reasonable time commitment. Do not seek private data without an appropriate basis. Accept no reply.

## Follow-up
Within the same thread if appropriate, remind the reader of the original request in one sentence and offer a new useful detail. A final note can acknowledge timing and close the loop. Never schedule follow-ups after a refusal or opt-out. A response requesting time or information requires a relevant, timely human reply, not another automated sequence.

## Example critiques
Generic 'I love your company' has no verifiable relevance; replace it with a sourced observation or remove it. 'We doubled every client's sales' needs proof and truthful qualification. 'Can we meet tomorrow?' may ask too much too early; consider offering a useful sample first. A job pitch should not use the same CTA as a partnership or service sale.
