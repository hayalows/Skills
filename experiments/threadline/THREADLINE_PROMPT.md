# Threadline 0.1: universal prompt template

This is an **independent, general-purpose** prompt, without any attached personal writing style. Replace the placeholders; do not imitate any source author.

```text
Write a [GENRE] for [READER].

The reader already knows: [WHAT THEY KNOW].
The reader needs: [PURPOSE OR QUESTION].
The central point that the facts warrant is: [CORE POINT].
The writer's stance and degree of certainty: [WARRANTED STANCE].
Length, if justified: [TARGET].
Verified information: [FACTS OR CITED SOURCE PACKET].
Unknown information: [UNKNOWNS].
Required details: [REQUIREMENTS].

Before drafting, identify the reader's actual question and create an internal map of the answer. Choose relevant reasons, causes, evidence, examples, consequences, fair objections and practical next steps. Do not force every element into a short message, and do not create a scene when none is justified.

Each paragraph should advance something already established, add a necessary explanation or introduce a genuine counterpoint. Keep the relationships between thoughts visible without inserting stock transition phrases. Repeat important terms when repetition helps continuity, but remove repeated claims that add no new meaning.

Use specific real details only when supplied; label illustrative examples as hypothetical. Do not invent personal memories, quotations, results, statistics, emotional experience or confidence. Match sentence structure to the thought: write briefly when directness helps, and use longer sentences for relationships that deserve to be explained. Do not engineer a particular readability grade or sentence-length pattern.

Fit the genre. A practical message should be easy to act on; an explainer should make the mechanism clear; an essay should test its own reasoning; a talk may use an earned recurring image. End when the reader has a sufficient answer or a meaningful next step. Avoid a mechanical recap.

Revise for factual support, concept consistency, movement from paragraph to paragraph, justified detail, appropriate emotional force and a fitting ending. Keep the finished prose only. Do not describe the writing method.
```

For revision, add the complete original text and explicitly request preservation of its meaning and level of certainty. For a neutral baseline comparison, use only the same brief and factual constraints without the editorial procedure.
