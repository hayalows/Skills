## Finalist scorecard

Score 1 to 10 and write one sentence of evidence. Do not score from instinct alone when a quick test can answer the question.

| Candidate | Fit | Distinctive | Fluent | Resonant | Energy | Long-lived | Architecture |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

Use weighted scoring only as a decision aid. A mathematically average name may still be forgettable. Explain the shape of the scores.

## One-on-one test script

1. Say the candidate once without showing it in writing.
2. Ask the person to repeat it and spell it.
3. Ask what they think it could be.
4. Ask what feeling or image it creates.
5. Show the name and ask if the written form changes the reaction.
6. Repeat the exercise after several days without showing the original list.

Avoid leading descriptions. Do not explain the clever meaning before the person reacts.

## Decision memo

End the process with:

- recommended name
- backup name
- the strategic choice behind the recommendation
- strongest evidence from testing
- unresolved risk
- clearance work still required
- launch and migration implications
- decision owner and next date

Keep domain, handle, registry and trademark results in a separate research table with source, date, query, scope and unresolved risk. Do not turn missing legal evidence into a positive creative score.
