# Name clearance and research

This is a research workflow, not legal advice. A clean-looking search result does not prove that a name is safe to use.

## Keep five checks separate

### 1. Domain

Search the exact spelling first, then sensible alternatives such as:

- `[name].com`
- `get[name].com`
- `[name]hq.com`
- `[name]app.com`
- a relevant country or category domain

Record whether a domain is unregistered, parked, actively used, for sale, redirected, or ambiguous. A parked domain may be negotiable, but that does not make the name strategically or legally safe.

### 2. Social handles

Check the platforms that matter for the audience: Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, X, Facebook, and any local channel that drives reach.

Record exact match, active use, inactive use, impersonation risk, and practical alternatives. An imperfect handle can be acceptable when the brand name is strong. Do not weaken the name just to obtain an exact handle.

### 3. Company or business registry

Check the jurisdiction where the entity will be formed. For Ghana, use the current official Registrar of Companies or relevant government source when the user asks about incorporation. For another country, use that country's official registry. Company-name registration and trademark rights are different questions.

Record the exact search term, spelling variants, result status, date, and whether the registry's rules require a formal name reservation or approval.

### 4. Trademark

Search the target jurisdiction and relevant markets. Use official databases where available, then widen the search to:

- exact spelling
- plural and singular forms
- phonetic variants
- common misspellings
- translations or transliterations
- related goods and services

For technology and business brands, classes often include software and SaaS, business services, finance, telecommunications, or education. Choose classes based on the actual offer, not a generic list.

Useful starting points include national trademark offices, the WIPO Global Brand Database, EUIPO, and USPTO when those jurisdictions matter. Verify current URLs before relying on them.

### 5. Language and culture

Check pronunciation, slang, offensive meanings, and unintended associations in each priority language. Ask a native speaker when the market matters. Translation tools can surface leads but are not enough for a final cultural judgment.

## Risk levels

| Finding | Working risk | Action |
|---|---:|---|
| No obvious result in a first-pass search | Unknown, not low by default | Widen the search and obtain legal review before launch |
| Similar name in an unrelated field | Medium | Compare audience, geography, goods, services, and visual similarity |
| Similar name in the same or adjacent field | High | Usually replace it unless counsel supports continued review |
| Exact active competitor or famous mark | Very high | Do not use |
| Descriptive or generic wording | Ownability risk | Expect weak exclusivity even if registration is possible |
| Domain is parked or for sale | Commercial risk | Investigate ownership, price, history, and redirects |
| Unclear cultural meaning | Market risk | Pause and obtain native-speaker feedback |

## Research log

For every finalist, keep:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Candidate | Exact spelling and capitalization |
| Date checked | UTC date or local date with timezone |
| Jurisdiction | Country or region |
| Source | Direct official or registrar URL |
| Search terms | Exact, phonetic, and variant terms |
| Result | What appeared, not a vague conclusion |
| Risk | Low, medium, high, or unknown with reason |
| Next action | Counsel, replace, negotiate, or test |

Never report "available" without saying which check was performed. Prefer language such as "no obvious exact result appeared in the sources checked on [date]; this is not clearance."
